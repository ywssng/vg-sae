# $L_0$ Is Not a Free Parameter: An Intuitive Summary and Paper Abstract

## Intuitive Research Summary

A neural activation vector can be pictured as a single image formed by stacking many transparent pieces. A sparse autoencoder (SAE) tries to recover a small set of latent pieces and use them to reconstruct that image. Reproducing the composite, however, is not the same as identifying the pieces that generated it. If the mean number of active latents, $L_0$, is set below the generating activity, correlated features can be merged into a single decoder direction; excessive $L_0$ can likewise produce duplicate or degenerate mixtures. Such representations may reconstruct well while recovering the wrong features. This work therefore treats $L_0$ not as an arbitrary sparsity budget, but as a model-dependent quantity to be inferred under an explicit definition of a feature.

The proposed Variational Garrote Sparse Autoencoder (VG-SAE) models every input–latent pair as a switch with a Bernoulli occupation probability and a deterministic nonnegative amplitude. Its objective combines posterior-mean reconstruction, the exact fluctuation cost induced by uncertain switches, a sparse-support prior, and posterior entropy in a variational free energy. In the statistical-mechanical interpretation, the sparsity coefficient $\gamma$ is an external field conjugate to occupation, the precision $\beta$ is an inverse-temperature-like noise precision, and mean occupation is an order parameter. Sweeping $\gamma$ produces posterior-mean soft and thresholded hard equations of state instead of imposing a fixed Top-$K$ count.

The implementation distinguishes learned and analytically profiled precision, posterior-mean and hard inference codes, and within-model posterior uncertainty from variation across independently trained models. Evaluation proceeds from controlled sparse coding with known ground truth to a pinned released SynthSAEBench artifact and, finally, to streamed language-model activations. Models are compared at measured hard $L_0$. Current synthetic evaluations record reconstruction, feature and support recovery, coverage, and decoder mixing; the confirmatory design adds cross-realization stability and causal diagnostics. Completed single-seed synthetic calibrations show systematic trade-offs among sparsity, reconstruction, and recovery, as well as substantial gaps between posterior-mean and thresholded inference behavior. The real-activation pipeline has been built for Gemma and Llama models, but its confirmatory experiments remain pending.

The central hypothesis is deliberately falsifiable: density-response and cross-realization uncertainty transitions should identify the feature-recovery regime even when reconstruction alone favors an incorrect representation. Current evidence does not yet establish that claim, multi-seed robustness, or uniform superiority to established SAE baselines. Moreover, $\gamma$ remains externally swept, amplitudes have neither a prior nor a posterior, and real activations provide no observable ground-truth dictionary or $L_0$. VG-SAE is therefore a sweep-based, model-conditional framework for testing whether an operating density can be inferred, not a parameter-free estimator of a universal number of features.

## Paper Abstract

Reproducing a superposition does not guarantee recovering its components. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are commonly selected on a sparsity–reconstruction frontier, treating the mean active-latent count, $L_0$, as a free operating point. At an incorrectly low $L_0$, an SAE can mix correlated features and reconstruct better than ground-truth directions at the same sparsity. We therefore treat $L_0$, conditional on a specified feature model and granularity, as an inferential target rather than a sparsity budget.

We introduce the Variational Garrote Sparse Autoencoder (VG-SAE), a statistical-mechanical formulation with input-dependent Bernoulli occupations and deterministic nonnegative amplitudes. A mean-field posterior defines a variational free-energy objective comprising expected Gaussian reconstruction energy, its exact diagonal Bernoulli fluctuation correction, a normalized sparse-support prior, posterior entropy, and the precision-dependent Gaussian normalization. The sparsity field $\gamma$ is conjugate to occupation, the precision $\beta$ is analogous to inverse temperature, and posterior mean occupation acts as an order parameter. Precision is either learned or analytically profiled. Sweeping $\gamma$ produces conditional soft and thresholded hard equations of state rather than imposing Top-$K$.

We evaluate VG-SAE on controlled sparse coding and in a 200-million-sample study using a pinned released SynthSAEBench artifact, comparing established SAELens baselines at measured hard $L_0$; we also provide a staged protocol for streamed language-model activations. Initial single-seed calibrations reveal systematic trade-offs among hard occupation, reconstruction, and recovery, together with substantial gaps between posterior-mean and thresholded codes. VG-SAE is competitive in selected low-density regimes but not uniformly superior to established baselines. Thus current evidence supports its diagnostic value, not automatic density identification. The decisive falsifiable hypothesis—that density-response and cross-realization uncertainty transitions co-localize with feature recovery even when reconstruction favors an incorrect representation—requires multi-seed confirmation. Because $\gamma$ remains external, amplitudes have neither a prior nor a posterior, and real activations provide no observable ground-truth dictionary or $L_0$, VG-SAE is a sweep-based, model-conditional framework rather than a parameter-free identifier.

**Keywords:** sparse autoencoders, Variational Garrote, feature recovery, variational free energy, statistical mechanics, interpretability
